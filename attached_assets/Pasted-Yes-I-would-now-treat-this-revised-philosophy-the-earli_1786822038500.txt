This is what makes ASHFALL different

The player controls one character.

The world controls itself.

You don’t command:

* armies
* workers
* cities
* settlements
* factions

Instead, those systems eventually exist around you.

A faction can go to war while you’re in a dungeon.

A village can become prosperous.

A mine can run out.

Wildlife can migrate.

Prices can change.

A road can become dangerous.

A settlement can grow.

You can influence those things through your actions, but you don’t directly control the simulation.

That is the Dwarf Fortress + Minecraft + classic RPG heart of ASHFALL.

Simulation LOD remains a core technology

We should absolutely keep your four levels.

Level 0 — Player bubble

Detailed simulation.

Level 1 — Nearby

Reduced simulation.

Level 2 — Distant

Abstract simulation.

Level 3 — Extremely distant

Statistical/event simulation.

Then:

abstract world → detailed world

when you approach.

That is how we can eventually pursue the enormous world without trying to simulate every NPC at maximum fidelity every frame.

One more important architectural rule

Your “dense data instead of thousands of objects” idea should become a design principle, not an absolute rule.

We shouldn’t force every system into arrays just because arrays sound faster.

Instead:

Choose the data representation based on measured access patterns, update frequency, memory behavior, and scalability.

Some things will naturally be dense data.

Some will be sparse.

Some will be objects.

Some will be events.

Some will be generated on demand.

Profile first.

That keeps the Java architecture clean while still pursuing the RollerCoaster Tycoon/Dwarf Fortress-style simulation efficiency we’re after.
